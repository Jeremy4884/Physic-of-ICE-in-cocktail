Zone refining in ice Ih

Ice on Earth crystallizes as hexagonal ice Ih. Its lattice has almost no room for foreign species, so the equilibrium partition coefficient for dissolved gases and mineral ions is extremely small:

Dissolved gases — O₂, N₂, CO₂k < 10−4
Mineral ions — Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Na⁺k < 10−4

Impurity rejection

As water molecules lock into the hexagonal network, gases and minerals are always pushed ahead of the freezing front into the remaining liquid. Whether they escape or get trapped depends on how fast the front moves and where the liquid can go.

Model: keff = k₀ + (1 − k₀)·v²/(v² + vc²), with k₀ = 10⁻⁴ and vc = 6 mm/h — a simplified Burton–Prim–Slichter-type rise of trapping with front speed. The remaining liquid follows a Scheil mass balance. Gas forms visible micro-voids once the liquid at the front becomes supersaturated. This is a teaching model, not a quantitative prediction.

Chilling & Dilution Kinetics

Shaking vs Stirring

Both techniques chill a drink the same way: by melting ice. There is no chilling without dilution, and no dilution without chilling. The difference is speed — shaking slams ice and liquid together so heat transfer is roughly an order of magnitude faster.

What the model shows

Shaking reaches near-equilibrium in about 10–15 s; shaking longer barely changes temperature or dilution. The drink ends close to its own freezing point (below 0 °C because of the ethanol and sugar) and is aerated — cloudy and textured.

Stirring transfers heat far more gently, so the curve keeps sloping. Real-world stirs (20–45 s) stop well before equilibrium, giving a warmer, less diluted, crystal-clear drink. Matching a 12 s shake takes roughly 1.5–2 minutes of stirring.

Warm vessels matter more for stirring: a room-temperature mixing glass (~500 g of glass) dumps heat into the drink, while a thin shaker tin holds little. Wet ice adds free surface water — dilution without chilling.

Model: lumped energy balance per time step. Heat flow Q = h·A·(Tdrink − Tfp), where Tfp is the freezing point of the current ethanol–sugar–water mix and A shrinks as the cubes melt (A ∝ m2/3). Ice warms to Tfp, then melts (334 J/g); meltwater joins the drink. The vessel and room exchange heat with the drink. h is calibrated so shaking plateaus in ~12 s and stirring needs ~2 min to match. Teaching model — real numbers depend on the cubes, the vessel and the bartender.
